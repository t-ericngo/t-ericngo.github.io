---
title: "Behind the Thrones: How Elite Networks Shape International Conflict Behavior of Autocracies"
status: working
order: 3
authors:
  - Anh-Tu Ngo
---

Why can leaders of seemingly institutionalized autocracies make costly decisions – such as starting international conflicts – despite being surrounded by politically aware elites who bear the consequences? This paper moves beyond leaders and formal institutions to the elites who create, maintain, and break those institutions. I argue that leaders face fewer constraints when elite networks are fragmented into disconnected factions, when the leader is highly central in the network, and when former officials ("old guards") lack the ties needed to punish the leader. Drawing on original data on the networks among current cabinet officials and retired elites, the paper examines whether fragmented and leader-centered networks make autocracies more prone to international conflict.
