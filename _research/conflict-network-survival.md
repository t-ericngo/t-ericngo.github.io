---
title: "Conflict, Network, and Survival: How Interstate and Intrastate Conflicts as well as Network Connections Influence Elite Tenure in Authoritarian Governments"
status: working
order: 2
authors:
  - Anh-Tu Ngo
---

How do interstate and intrastate conflicts reshape the ruling coalitions of authoritarian regimes? This paper examines how conflict and elites' network connections jointly shape elite tenure in autocratic cabinets. Using original data on the careers, education, and family ties of cabinet elites (1990–2024), I find that, relative to non-conflict periods, civil conflicts increase elites' risk of cabinet exit, while international conflicts reduce it. Compared to less connected elites, well-connected elites are less likely to exit the cabinet during civil conflicts, yet more vulnerable during international ones. Meanwhile, direct ties to former elites – and ties to the leader during international conflicts – are protective. In other words, leaders may rely on well-connected elites during civil conflicts, while international conflicts may allow leaders to remove them while sparing their own associates. The protective effect of ties to old guards, however, suggests limits to how far leaders can remake their ruling coalitions.
