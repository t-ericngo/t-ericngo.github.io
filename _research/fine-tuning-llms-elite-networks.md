---
title: "Fine-Tuning Open LLMs for Complex Information Extraction in Low-Resource Settings: Building Elite Networks from Biographies"
status: working
order: 1
authors:
  - Anh-Tu Ngo
---

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) offer new opportunities for political scientists to transform large corpora of texts into structured data that enables substantively interesting analyses. Nevertheless, many important questions in political science require complex information that is relational, hierarchical, and temporally dynamic: who the actors are (individuals, groups, or states), what attributes they have, what actions they take, which events they are involved in, and how they relate to one another over time. Extracting such information goes beyond classification of documents or estimation of their semantics and remains challenging even for powerful LLMs. This creates a cycle of data scarcity: the lack of structured data motivates the creation of automated extraction tools, yet training these tools requires the very same data that are lacking. This paper introduces a codebook-to-JSON pipeline for structured information extraction (IE) in such low-resource settings – converting a codebook into a nested output template, hand-annotating a few hundred documents, augmenting them with synthetic examples, fine-tuning open LLMs for extraction tasks, and evaluating their outputs. Applied to biographies of cabinet elites in Vietnam, Russia, and Cambodia, this pipeline produced a fine-tuned Mistral Small 3.2 (24B) that matches or even outperforms some of the most powerful proprietary LLMs (GPT 5.6 Luna and Sol and Gemini 3.7 Flash). The fine-tuned model can reliably extract nested and complicated career records with few missing or hallucinated entries, enabling the construction and analysis of elite networks across countries and years. Although demonstrated on elite biographies, the pipeline offers a generalizable IE strategy for building structured political datasets from complex texts where labeled data is scarce.
