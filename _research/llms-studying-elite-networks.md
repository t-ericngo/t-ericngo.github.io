Large language models (LLMs) increasingly enable automated extraction of political networks from text across regimes, languages, and source types. However, differences between systems in entity and relationship definitions, sources, decisions delegated to LLMs, and evaluation limit direct comparison of extracted data and reported performance. This limited comparability hinders researchers from building on each other's work and identifying methodological advances. This paper takes a first step towards finding common ground by synthesising nine project descriptions contributed by the authors with four independent applications and related methods research, spanning democratic and authoritarian regimes, multiple languages, and sources ranging from news archives to court records. Beyond reviewing the field, the paper proposes as its main contribution a four-axis taxonomy—entity, relationship, source, and LLM delegation—to compare extraction systems and guide researchers in designing pipelines suited to their research questions. It also argues that since structural bottlenecks like entity resolution and cross-system comparability exceed the capacity of single teams, the field requires shared standards and a coordinated infrastructure. For that purpose, it proposes an add-on to GRAND (Guidelines for Reporting About Network Data) standards specifying how LLM-based extraction should be documented.
