Welcome! I am T. Eric Ngo – or Anh-Tu, my Vietnamese name and the name I publish under – a PhD candidate in Political Science at the University of Iowa. My research examines how political elites and domestic politics shape – and are shaped by – political violence within and between states. It follows three connected lines. The first, which anchors my dissertation (*Webs of Power*), asks how networks among cabinet officials and retired elites constrain or enable autocrats to pursue costly choices such as purges, repression, and international conflict, and how conflict in turn reshapes those networks. The second examines the domestic sources of international conflict, from how ethnic inequality pushes leaders toward diversionary conflict to how economic conditions lead autocracies to militarize maritime claims. The third studies how great powers compete for influence through foreign aid, and how recipient states' domestic politics shape US–China aid competition, including in artificial intelligence.

To study elite networks, I built an original dataset of the careers, education, family ties, and relationships of cabinet elites from 1990 to 2024 – fine-grained, relational detail that existing cross-national datasets lack. A system of large language model (LLM) agents searches for, cross-checks, and compiles multilingual sources into biographies, and fine-tuned open-source LLMs turn those biographies into structured records from which networks can be built. I am now carrying this approach into new questions: with colleagues at the Disaster, Migration, and Violence Lab, I study how elite politics, political crises, and electoral violence shape government responses to natural disasters, and I am building a database of provincial elites in Vietnam and their ties to national patrons. My work also draws on the politics and international relations of Southeast Asia, where I am from: elite politics in Vietnam and Cambodia informs my theories and data, my research on maritime disputes and US–China competition speaks directly to the region, and my co-authored work examines nationalism and political participation in Vietnam's digital sphere.

## Background

I received my M.A. in Political Science from the University of Iowa in 2023 and my B.S. in Political Science and International Relations from Truman State University, Missouri, in 2021. I am also completing additional training in computational social science at the Swedish Research School in Computational Social Science (SIRCSS), Linköping University. Methodologically, I combine statistical methods – generalized linear models, time-series analysis, and multilevel modeling – with computational tools such as LLMs, machine learning, text analysis, and network analysis, working in R, Python, and Stata.

I am a proud native of Vietnam. Coming from a central province of Vietnam and now studying in the US Midwest, it seems I'm fated to reside almost exclusively in the middle regions of the countries I've lived in (interestingly, my surname in Vietnamese means "corn", a major agricultural product of Iowa and Missouri).
