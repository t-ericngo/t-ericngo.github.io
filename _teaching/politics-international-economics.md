The course introduces students to the deep interactions between global economics and international politics. It focuses on the historical roots of international political economy (IPE), state cooperation, global/regional trade, and international finance. The course also covers globalization, state conflict, environmental issues, economic inequality, and global development. I assisted Nick Martini in running in-class discussions and activities that connected these topics to everyday examples, and graded students' assignments.
